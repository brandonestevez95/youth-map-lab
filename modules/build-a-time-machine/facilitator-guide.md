# Facilitator Guide: Build a Time Machine 🕰️

Welcome to the *Build a Time Machine* workshop — a humanities + GIS learning experience where students reconstruct Austin’s past, imagine its future, and discover how maps let us “time travel” through history.  

**Audience**: Ages 12–17  
**Length**: 30 minutes (extendable to 60 minutes)  
**Format**: Mostly paper-based, with a short digital demo  
**Skills Covered**: Spatial thinking, historical imagination, civic design, GIS literacy  

---

## 🧰 Pre-Workshop Setup

### ✅ Digital Prep
- Open ArcGIS Online with Austin basemaps ready:
  - Historical imagery or Sanborn Fire Insurance maps (if available)
  - Current aerials and infrastructure
- Confirm you can toggle between historic and present-day layers.

## 📅 Workshop Agenda

### 1. 🌆 Introduction (5 min)
- Facilitator quick intro: names & why mapping = time travel.  
- Show 2–3 images of Austin:  
  - 1900s (horses + dirt roads)  
  - 1970s (Sixth Street live music)  
  - 2020s (skyline + tech towers)  
- Ask: “What’s different? What’s the same?” → get 1–2 responses.  
- Transition: “We’re going to build a time machine today using maps.”  

---

### 2. 🗺 Activity 1: Mapping the Past (8 min)
- Hand out blank Austin maps.  
- Prompt: “It’s 1920. Where do people gather? Where are farms, railroads, or music halls?”  
- Provide context: cotton industry, rail lines, segregation, music culture.  
- Task: Students draw/label at least 3 features on their “1920 Austin.”  
- Optional: Each group names their “time traveler’s Austin” & shares one highlight.  

---

### 3. 💬 Reflection & Discussion (5 min)
- Guiding questions (popcorn style):  
  - “What surprised you about 1920s Austin?”  
  - “What would be hardest about living there?”  
  - “What parts of Austin today trace back to then?”  
- Key point: Cities evolve, but history leaves fingerprints (railroads, neighborhoods, cultural hubs).  

---

### 4. 🚀 Activity 2: Mapping the Future (8 min)
- Hand out fresh blank maps.  
- Prompt: “Jump to 2070. Austin is your city of the future. Add at least 3 features.”  
- Provide prompts: driverless highways, climate refugees, green energy hubs, space launchpads, AI schools.  
- Output: Draw, label, or doodle ideas.  
- Optional: 1-minute group pitch of their future Austin.  

---

### 5. 🌐 AGOL Demo (5 min)
- Pull up ArcGIS Online map of Austin.  
- Show: toggle between historic basemaps and present-day aerials.  
- Frame it: “GIS is professional time travel — it lets us analyze past patterns, present growth, and future planning.”  
- Closing thought: “The past shapes the present, and the present shapes the future. You’ve just built your own time machine.”  

---

## ✅ Facilitator Tips
- Keep instructions short, playful, and curiosity-driven.  
- Encourage creativity over “accuracy” — let doodles, symbols, and wild ideas flourish.  
- Use reflection to tie back to real urban planning & history.  
- If time runs long: merge reflection into the AGOL demo, or cut group pitches short.  

---

## 📝 Paper-Based Only Option
If no tech is available, skip the AGOL demo. Instead, use the printed maps and photos to close with a “gallery walk” of past & future Austins.

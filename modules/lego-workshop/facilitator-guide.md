# Facilitator Guide: LEGO Play 🧱 Urban Planning Edition

Welcome to *LEGO Play: Urban Planning* — a hands-on design workshop where youth explore how cities work, build their own LEGO cityscapes, and reflect on the power of infrastructure, services, and zoning to shape everyday life.

**Audience**: Ages 13–17  
**Length**: 45 minutes (extendable to 60)  
**Format**: Hands-on building with LEGO, light discussion, optional GIS/AI demo  
**Skills Covered**: City planning, teamwork, spatial reasoning, civic imagination  

---

## 🧰 Pre-Workshop Setup

### ✅ Physical Setup
- One **gray baseplate** per group (3–5 students)
- Distribute LEGO pieces into trays or piles per table (see parts list below)
- Reserve one corner or wall for your **mock “social media wall”** — use sticky notes or whiteboard for playful public feedback.

### 🧱 LEGO Materials

| Type           | Use                      | Colors                             |
|----------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| 2x4 + 2x2 Bricks | Walls/buildings         | Red, White, Blue, Brown             |
| Glass Frames   | Windows, stores           | Transparent                         |
| Plates         | Parks, lawns, rooftops    | Green                               |
| Flat Tiles     | Roads, sidewalks          | Dark Gray, Black                    |
| Special Pieces | Trees, signs, roofs       | Any                                 |

### 🗂 Deliverables Per Group
Each city **must include**:
- 🚒 Fire Station  
- 🚓 Police Station  
- 🏘️ Homes  
- 🌳 Park  

If time permits:
- 🏥 Hospital  
- 🏢 Extra services or businesses  

---

## 📅 Workshop Agenda

### 1. 🌆 Introduction (5 min)

**Opening Prompt**:  
“Have you ever thought about *why* cities look the way they do? Why some neighborhoods have parks, hospitals, or fire stations—and others don’t?”

**Facilitator Demo**:  
- Brief explanation of zoning (residential, commercial, civic)  
- Mention city services: “What makes a city livable?”  
- Show a real-world map or aerial photo to highlight layout and functions.

**Instructions Overview**:
> “You’re going to build your own mini city out of LEGO. But your city needs to be safe, livable, and interesting.”

---

### 2. 🏗️ Main Activity: Build Your City (20–25 min)

**Teams of 3–5 students** work around a shared baseplate.  
They will:
- Designate zones (housing, civic, green space)
- Build at least one of each required facility  
- Add detail using color (parks, roads, services)

#### 🧠 Twist: Mock Social Media Wall  
Facilitators walk around and “post” fake community complaints, suggestions, or praise. Examples:
- “Group 3: Love the park, but no one can get to it — no sidewalks!”  
- “Group 1: My house burned down. No fire department?!”  
- “Group 2: Great traffic flow, but where’s the hospital?”

**Goal**: Prompt teams to adapt, redesign, or defend their choices.

#### Optional Extensions
- Add elevation rules: “No building can be taller than 3 bricks without a permit.”  
- Introduce population pressure mid-game: “10 new families just moved in. Add housing!”

---

### 3. 💬 Reflection & Debrief (5–7 min)

Gather teams for quickfire discussion. Ask:
- “What worked well in your city?”  
- “What was hardest to plan for?”  
- “If you had 15 more minutes, what would you change or add?”  
- “What do you think is *most* important in a city—safety, fun, housing, or something else?”

**Key Takeaways**:
- Tradeoffs between beauty, utility, and safety  
- How missing services affect people  
- Importance of civic infrastructure  

---

### 4. 🌐 AGOL / AI Demo (Optional – 5–10 min)

If tech is available, transition into a **live map demo**:
- Show a real city in ArcGIS Online  
- Turn on layers:
  - Zoning districts  
  - Infrastructure (fire stations, hospitals, etc.)  
  - Parks and green space

#### Framing:
> “Urban planners use tools like GIS to build cities that are safe, fair, and functional. Today, you just did that with LEGO—but planners do it with data, maps, and community voices.”

---

## ✅ Facilitator Tips

- Focus on process, not perfection  
- Use the “social media wall” to spark critical thinking  
- Celebrate wild ideas—skyparks, floating hospitals, etc.  
- If students finish early: add population, traffic, or “disaster planning”

---

## 📝 Paper-Based Backup Option

If LEGO bricks are unavailable:
- Use grid paper and colored pencils  
- Optional: cutouts of buildings, roads, trees  
- Still use social media wall and zoning reflection

---

## 🔗 Optional Extensions

- Round 2 with a twist (e.g. flood-prone coastal town, or college campus)  
- Run a mayoral “election” where teams pitch their cities  
- Take photos of each city and upload to a digital StoryMap
